Black-hole thermodynamics and Hawking radiation

A black hole is not just a sink — it has temperature, entropy, and a finite lifetime. This page derives the four laws of black-hole mechanics, the Hawking temperature, the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, and the evaporation timescale.

1. The four laws

  • Zeroth law: the surface gravity κ is constant over the horizon (analogous to uniform temperature).
  • First law: dM = (κ/8πG) dA + Ω dJ + Φ dQ (mass = energy; area = entropy).
  • Second law: the horizon area never decreases: dA ≥ 0 (Hawking's area theorem).
  • Third law: κ → 0 cannot be reached in finite steps (extremal limit is unattainable).

2. Hawking temperature

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime gives a black hole a thermal spectrum with temperature

$$ T_{\rm H} = \frac{\hbar c^{3}}{8\pi G M k_{\rm B}} \approx 6.17\times10^{-8}\,\frac{M_{\odot}}{M}\ {\rm K} $$

For a solar-mass hole this is ~60 nK — unobservably cold. For a 1012 kg (asteroid-mass) hole it is ~1011 K, comparable to the CMB-era universe.

3. Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

The entropy is proportional to the horizon area, not the volume:

$$ S_{\rm BH} = \frac{k_{\rm B} c^{3} A}{4 G \hbar} = \frac{k_{\rm B} c^{3}}{4 G \hbar}\,4\pi r_{s}^{2} = \frac{4\pi k_{\rm B} G M^{2}}{\hbar c} $$

In Planck units (kB = 1) this is S = A/4 in Planck areas — one quarter of the horizon area. A solar-mass hole has S ≈ 1077 kB, vastly larger than any ordinary object of the same mass, which is the origin of the information paradox.

4. Evaporation timescale

A hole radiates as a black body of area A = 4π rs2 and temperature TH. Equating dM/dt = −L/c2 gives

$$ t_{\rm evap} = \frac{5120\pi G^{2} M^{3}}{\hbar c^{4}} \approx 2.1\times10^{67}\left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{3}\ {\rm yr} $$

For M = M⊙ this is ~1067 yr, far longer than the age of the universe. Only holes below ~1015 g (asteroid mass) could have evaporated by now; such primordial holes would be ending their lives today in bursts of gamma rays.

5. Specific heat and the negative-capacity problem

The heat capacity C = dM/dT is negative:

$$ C = \frac{dM}{dT_{\rm H}} = -\frac{8\pi G M^{2} k_{\rm B}}{\hbar c^{3}} < 0 $$

A black hole that loses mass gets hotter, so it radiates faster — a runaway that ends in a final burst. This negative heat capacity is why a hole cannot be in stable equilibrium with a heat bath; it either grows or evaporates.

6. The information paradox (briefly)

Hawking radiation is thermal, so if a hole evaporates completely the final state is a thermal mixed state regardless of what fell in. This conflicts with unitary quantum evolution. Proposed resolutions include: complementarity, the firewall, soft hair (Hawking-Perry-Strominger), and the island formula. None is settled; the paradox remains the central open problem in quantum gravity.

7. Assumptions and caveats

  • Semiclassical: the metric is treated classically, quantum effects only in the matter fields. Breaks down near the Planck mass.
  • Schwarzschild (non-rotating, uncharged). Kerr holes have a more complex emission spectrum (grey-body factors depend on (l, m, spin)).
  • The evaporation formula assumes no accretion and no mergers; real holes grow by accretion, which dominates for astrophysical masses.
  • All quantities are derived or model-derived; Hawking radiation has not been observed.

8. References
