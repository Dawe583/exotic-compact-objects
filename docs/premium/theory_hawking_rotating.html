Hawking radiation from rotating black holes

A Kerr black hole radiates, but its spectrum is not a simple blackbody. The spin, the superradiant modes, and the grey-body factors all modify the Hawking emission.

1. Temperature of a Kerr hole

The surface gravity of a Kerr hole is

$$ \kappa = \frac{r_{+}-r_{-}}{2(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})} = \frac{\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}}}{2M(M+\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}})} $$

so the Hawking temperature is TH = ℏκ/(2πkBc). For a = 0 this reduces to the Schwarzschild value 6.17×10⁻⁸ (M⊙/M) K. As a → M (extremal), κ → 0 and TH → 0.

2. Superradiance

For rotating holes, modes with frequency ω < mΩH (where m is the azimuthal quantum number and ΩH the horizon angular velocity) are amplified rather than absorbed. This is superradiant scattering (Zel'dovich 1971). It leads to:

  • Amplification of waves scattered off the hole (the wave analogue of the Penrose process).
  • Instability of massive boson fields around rotating holes (the "black-hole bomb").
  • A non-thermal correction to the Hawking spectrum at low frequencies.

3. Grey-body factors

The emission is not that of a perfect black body: the potential barrier around the hole (the "centrifugal barrier") reflects low-frequency modes back into the hole. The grey-body factor Γωlm is the transmission coefficient. The power emitted in mode (ω, l, m) is

$$ \frac{dE}{dt\,d\omega} = \\frac{\\hbar}{2\\pi} \\frac{\\Gamma_{\\omega l m}}{e^{\\hbar(\\omega-m\\Omega_H)/k_B T_H}-1} $$

For a Schwarzschild hole this reduces to the standard Hawking formula with Γ as a frequency-dependent correction.

4. Evaporation of a Kerr hole

A rotating hole loses both mass and angular momentum. The spin decreases faster than the mass for most of the evolution, so the hole tends to spin down before evaporating. The final state (as M → 0) is a burst of high-energy particles. The total lifetime is still ~M³ (same as Schwarzschild to leading order), but the spectrum is harder at early times.

5. Assumptions and caveats

  • Semiclassical approximation; breaks down near Planck mass.
  • The grey-body factors require numerical solution of the Teukolsky equation; the formulas here are schematic.
  • Superradiant instabilities require a massive boson; for massless fields (photons, gravitons) there is no instability, only amplification.
