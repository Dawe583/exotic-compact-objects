Kerr geodesics and frame dragging

The Kerr metric describes a rotating black hole. This page derives the geodesic equations, the ISCO as a function of spin, and the Lense-Thirring (frame-dragging) effect.

1. The Kerr metric

In Boyer-Lindquist coordinates (t, r, θ, φ):

$$ ds^{2} = -\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right)dt^{2} - \frac{4Mar\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\,dt\,d\phi + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}dr^{2} + \Sigma\,d\theta^{2} + \left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2Ma^{2}r\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2} $$

with Σ = r² + a² cos²θ, Δ = r² − 2Mr + a², and a = J/M (spin parameter, 0 ≤ a ≤ M). The horizons are at r± = M ± √(M² − a²).

2. Conserved quantities

By axisymmetry and stationarity there are three conserved quantities per unit mass: energy E, angular momentum Lz, and the Carter constant Q. The geodesic equations reduce to first-order ODEs in r and θ:

$$ \Sigma\,\frac{dr}{d\lambda} = \pm\sqrt{R(r)},\qquad \Sigma\,\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = \pm\sqrt{\Theta(\theta)} $$

where R(r) and Θ(θ) are quartic polynomials in r and θ built from E, Lz, Q. This is what makes Kerr geodesics integrable (Carter 1968).

3. ISCO as a function of spin

The innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) radius depends on spin and on whether the orbit is prograde or retrograde:

$$ r_{\rm ISCO} = M\left\{3 + Z_{2} \mp \sqrt{(3-Z_{1})(3+Z_{1}+2Z_{2})}\right\} $$

with Z1 = 1 + (1−a²/M²)1/3[(1+a/M)1/3 + (1−a/M)1/3] and Z2 = √(3a²/M² + Z1²). The minus sign is prograde, the plus is retrograde.

  • a = 0 (Schwarzschild): rISCO = 6 M (prograde = retrograde).
  • a = M (extremal): rISCO,pro = M, rISCO,retro = 9 M.

This site's kerr_isco figure plots both branches; the original code had a factor-of-two bug (12 GM/c² instead of 6 GM/c²) that was found and fixed.

4. Frame dragging (Lense-Thirring)

A rotating mass drags inertial frames. The precession frequency of a gyroscope at radius r is

$$ \Omega_{\rm LT} = \frac{2 G J}{c^{2} r^{3}} = \frac{2 G M a}{c^{2} r^{3}} $$

For the Earth (a ≈ 0) this is tiny (~10-14 rad/s at the surface); Gravity Probe B measured it to ~1%. For a Kerr hole near the ISCO, frame dragging is of order c/r and dominates the dynamics.

5. Observational tests

  • X-ray reflection spectroscopy: the iron Kα line profile depends on rISCO and hence on spin; this is the main spin-measurement technique for stellar-mass holes.
  • Continuum fitting: the disk temperature profile depends on rISCO; used for stellar-mass holes with known mass and distance.
  • Gravitational waves: the spin-orbit coupling in the inspiral imprints on the waveform; the effective spin χeff is measured for every merger.
  • EHT: the shadow shape constrains spin (see the EHT shadow page).

5b. Effective potential and zoom-whirl orbits

For equatorial geodesics (Q = 0) the radial motion reduces to (dr/dτ)² = E² − Veff(r), with

$$ V_{\rm eff}(r) = \frac{L_{z}^{2}}{r^{2}} - \frac{2M}{r}\left(1 + \frac{L_{z}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right) + \frac{a^{2}}{r^{2}}(E^{2}-1) + \frac{2Ma}{r^{3}}(2EL_{z} - a^{2}E^{2}/r) $$

The shape of Veff determines the orbit type:

  • Circular: Veff = E² and dVeff/dr = 0 (stable if d²V/dr² > 0).
  • Plunge: the particle crosses the horizon when Veff < E² at small r.
  • Zoom-whirl: for eccentric orbits near the separatrix, the particle zooms in from apoapsis, whirls around the hole many times near periapsis, then zooms out. The number of whirls grows logarithmically as the orbit approaches the separatrix.

Zoom-whirl orbits are the strong-field signature of eccentric EMRIs (extreme mass-ratio inspirals) and are a key LISA target: their harmonic structure encodes the spin and mass to high precision.

6. Assumptions and caveats

  • Vacuum Kerr metric; ignores accretion disk back-reaction.
  • Test particles (geodesic approximation); finite-size and radiation-reaction effects matter near the ISCO.
  • Spin measurements depend on disk models (inclination, emissivity) that are not fully understood.
