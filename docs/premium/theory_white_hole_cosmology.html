White holes as cosmological objects

If white holes exist, where would we find them? This page collects the cosmological and observational constraints: primordial white holes, the Big Bang as a white hole, and possible signatures.

1. The Big Bang as a white hole

The maximal (Kruskal) extension of Schwarzschild contains a white-hole region in the past. In cosmology, the time-reversed analogue is the Big Bang itself: a past singularity from which the universe expands. In this reading the entire universe is the interior of a white hole, and the "horizon" is the particle horizon. This is suggestive but not a prediction — it assumes spherical symmetry and no matter, both false for our universe.

2. Primordial white holes

If primordial black holes (PBHs) formed in the early universe, their time-reversed counterparts (primordial white holes) are equally allowed by the equations. They would have masses from the Planck mass to ~105 M⊙ and would decay on the instability timescale (see the instability page), i.e. essentially instantaneously for astrophysical masses. Hence primordial white holes are not expected to survive to the present.

3. Observational signatures

  • Gamma-ray bursts: a white-hole burst would resemble a short GRB with no progenitor and no afterglow; no such population has been identified.
  • Fast radio bursts: proposed (but not established) as white-hole signatures; the dispersion measures and repetition statistics do not favour this.
  • CMB: a population of primordial white holes would leave an imprint on the CMB; Planck constraints on PBHs apply symmetrically.
  • Gravitational waves: a white-hole "explosion" would produce a burst-like signal distinct from a merger; LIGO/Virgo have not identified one.

4. The arrow of time problem

White holes require a past singularity with low entropy, i.e. a finely tuned initial state. The observed arrow of time (entropy increases) makes white holes exponentially improbable in any realistic cosmology. This is the deeper reason they are not seen: not just instability, but thermodynamic improbability.

5. Assumptions and caveats

  • All statements are theoretical; no white hole has been detected.
  • The Big-Bang-as-white-hole reading is a heuristic, not a model.
  • Primordial white holes inherit all the constraints (and problems) of primordial black holes, plus the instability.

6. References

  • Hawking S. W., Ellis G. F. R., 1973, The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time.
  • Carr B. J., Hawking S. W., 1974, MNRAS 168, 399 — primordial black holes.
  • Rovelli C., Vidotto F., 2014, Int. J. Mod. Phys. D 23, 1442026 — Planck stars and white holes.
