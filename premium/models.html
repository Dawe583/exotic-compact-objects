Home/Models

Six geometries that organise the programme.

Each model is implemented symbolically or numerically, checked against a known limit, and connected to an observable.

Canonical

Schwarzschild geometry

The unique spherically symmetric vacuum solution. Sets the horizon scale Rs = 2GM/c2 and the reference against which every compact object is measured.

$$ ds^2 = -\left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)c^2dt^2 + \left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2 $$

Rotating

Kerr spacetime & ISCO

Frame dragging, the ergosphere and the spin-dependent innermost stable circular orbit. The ISCO sets the radiative efficiency of accretion and the cut-off of the remnant spectrum.

$$ r_{\rm ISCO} = \frac{GM}{c^2}\left[3+Z_2 \mp \sqrt{(3-Z_1)(3+Z_1+2Z_2)}\right] $$
Read the derivation →
Exotic

Morris-Thorne wormhole

A static, spherically symmetric throat supported by exotic matter. The flare-out condition and the null-energy-condition violation quantify how much exotic stress-energy is required.

$$ ds^2 = -e^{2\Phi(r)}c^2dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{1-b(r)/r} + r^2d\Omega^2 $$
Read the derivation →
Instability

White-hole horizon

The time reverse of gravitational collapse. Perturbations grow without bound near the horizon, driving mass ejection and a runaway instability.

$$ \delta(r,t) \propto e^{\kappa t}\, e^{-(r-r_h)^2/2\sigma^2} $$
Read the derivation →
Inference

Gravitational-wave Fisher matrix

A local Gaussian approximation to the likelihood. Diagonalises parameter correlations and forecasts how well mass, spin and distance can be measured.

$$ \Gamma_{ij} = \left\langle \frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta_i}, \frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta_j} \right\rangle, \qquad \sigma_i \ge \sqrt{(\Gamma^{-1})_{ii}} $$
Read the derivation →
Geometry

Raychaudhuri focusing

The evolution of a congruence of geodesics. Expansion, shear and vorticity control whether light rays focus, which underpins the singularity theorems.

$$ \frac{d\theta}{d\tau} = -\frac{1}{3}\theta^2 - \sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab} + \omega_{ab}\omega^{ab} - R_{ab}u^a u^b $$
