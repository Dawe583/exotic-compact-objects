Black Hole Perturbation Theory: Complete Theoretical Derivation

1. Introduction and Setup

Black hole perturbation theory studies linearized fluctuations around a black hole background. For Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes, the perturbations separate into decoupled master equations governing gravitational, electromagnetic, and scalar perturbations.

1.1 Background Metric

Schwarzschild metric: \begin{equation} ds^2 = -f(r) dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f(r)} + r^2 d\Omega^2, \quad f(r) = 1 - \frac{2M}{r} \end{equation}

Kerr metric (Boyer-Lindquist): \begin{align} ds^2 &= -\frac{\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma} dt^2 - \frac{2a\sin^2\theta(r^2+a^2-\Delta)}{\Sigma} dt d\phi \\ &\quad + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta} dr^2 + \Sigma d\theta^2 + \frac{(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma} \sin^2\theta d\phi^2 \end{align} with \(\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2\), \(\Sigma = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta\).

1.2 Perturbation Ansatz

Metric perturbation: \(g_{\mu\nu} \to g_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}\). Electromagnetic perturbation: \(F_{\mu\nu} \to F_{\mu\nu} + \delta F_{\mu\nu}\). Scalar perturbation: \(\Phi \to \Phi + \delta\Phi\).

Decompose into tensor spherical harmonics (Schwarzschild) or spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics (Kerr).

2. Schwarzschild Perturbations: Regge-Wheeler-Zerilli Formalism

2.1 Tensor Harmonic Decomposition

Perturbations split into axial (odd) and polar (even) sectors under parity \(\theta \to \pi-\theta\), \(\phi \to \phi+\pi\).

#### Axial (Odd) Perturbations \begin{equation} h_{\mu\nu}^{\text{odd}} = \sum_{\ell m} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -h_0 \csc\theta \partial_\phi Y_{\ell m} & h_0 \sin\theta \partial_\theta Y_{\ell m} \\ 0 & 0 & -h_1 \csc\theta \partial_\phi Y_{\ell m} & h_1 \sin\theta \partial_\theta Y_{\ell m} \\ \text{sym} & \text{sym} & h_2 \mathcal{X}_{\theta\phi} & \frac{1}{2} h_2 (\mathcal{X}_{\theta\theta} - \mathcal{X}_{\phi\phi}) \\ \text{sym} & \text{sym} & \frac{1}{2} h_2 (\mathcal{X}_{\theta\theta} - \mathcal{X}_{\phi\phi}) & -h_2 \mathcal{X}_{\theta\phi} \end{pmatrix} \end{equation} where \(\mathcal{X}_{ab}\) are the axial tensor harmonics.

#### Polar (Even) Perturbations \begin{equation} h_{\mu\nu}^{\text{even}} = \sum_{\ell m} \begin{pmatrix} H_0 Y_{\ell m} & H_1 Y_{\ell m} & h_0 \partial_\theta Y_{\ell m} & h_0 \partial_\phi Y_{\ell m} \\ H_1 Y_{\ell m} & H_2 Y_{\ell m} & h_1 \partial_\theta Y_{\ell m} & h_1 \partial_\phi Y_{\ell m} \\ \text{sym} & \text{sym} & r^2(K Y_{\ell m} + G \partial_\theta^2 Y_{\ell m}) & r^2 G (\partial_\theta\partial_\phi - \cot\theta \partial_\phi) Y_{\ell m} \\ \text{sym} & \text{sym} & \text{sym} & r^2\sin^2\theta (K Y_{\ell m} + G \partial_\phi^2 Y_{\ell m}/\sin^2\theta) \end{pmatrix} \end{equation}

2.2 Regge-Wheeler Equation (Axial)

For axial perturbations, the master variable is: \begin{equation} \Psi_{\text{RW}} = \frac{r}{\lambda} \left( h_1 - \frac{r}{2} \partial_r h_0 \right), \quad \lambda = \frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)(\ell+2) \end{equation}

The Regge-Wheeler equation: \begin{equation} \boxed{\frac{d^2 \Psi_{\text{RW}}}{dr_*^2} + \left[ \omega^2 - V_{\text{RW}}(r) \right] \Psi_{\text{RW}} = 0} \end{equation}

where \(dr_* = dr/f(r)\) is the tortoise coordinate, and the potential is: \begin{equation} V_{\text{RW}}(r) = f(r) \left[ \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} - \frac{6M}{r^3} \right] \end{equation}

2.3 Zerilli Equation (Polar)

For polar perturbations, the Zerilli master variable: \begin{equation} \Psi_{\text{Z}} = \frac{r}{\lambda+1} \left[ \frac{r^2}{\Lambda r + 3M} \left( K + \frac{r-2M}{r} (H_2 - r \partial_r K) \right) \right] \end{equation} where \(\Lambda = \frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)(\ell+2) = \lambda\).

The Zerilli equation: \begin{equation} \boxed{\frac{d^2 \Psi_{\text{Z}}}{dr_*^2} + \left[ \omega^2 - V_{\text{Z}}(r) \right] \Psi_{\text{Z}} = 0} \end{equation}

with potential: \begin{equation} V_{\text{Z}}(r) = \frac{2f(r)}{r^3} \frac{9M^3 + 3\Lambda M^2 r + \Lambda^2(\Lambda+1) M r^2 + \Lambda^2(\Lambda+1) r^3/3}{(3M + \Lambda r)^2} \end{equation}

2.4 Isospectrality

Remarkably, \(V_{\text{RW}}\) and \(V_{\text{Z}}\) are isospectral: they have the same quasi-normal mode spectrum. They are related by a Darboux transformation: \begin{equation} V_{\text{Z}} = V_{\text{RW}} + \frac{2f}{r} \frac{d}{dr} \left( \frac{12Mr - 6M^2}{r(3M+\Lambda r)} \right) \end{equation}

3. Electromagnetic and Scalar Perturbations on Schwarzschild

3.1 Scalar Field

Massless scalar field \(\Phi\): \begin{equation} \Box \Phi = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{d^2 \Psi}{dr_*^2} + \left[ \omega^2 - V_{\text{scalar}}(r) \right] \Psi = 0 \end{equation} \begin{equation} V_{\text{scalar}}(r) = f(r) \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} \end{equation}

3.2 Electromagnetic Field

Maxwell perturbations split into axial (magnetic) and polar (electric) modes. Both satisfy: \begin{equation} \frac{d^2 \Psi_{\text{EM}}}{dr_*^2} + \left[ \omega^2 - V_{\text{EM}}(r) \right] \Psi_{\text{EM}} = 0 \end{equation} \begin{equation} V_{\text{EM}}(r) = f(r) \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} \end{equation}

Same as scalar! (For Schwarzschild, EM and scalar potentials coincide.)

4. Kerr Perturbations: Teukolsky Formalism

4.1 Newman-Penrose Formalism

Use Kinnersley null tetrad: \begin{align} l^\mu &= \frac{1}{\Delta}(r^2+a^2, \Delta, 0, a) \\ n^\mu &= \frac{1}{2\Sigma}(r^2+a^2, -\Delta, 0, a) \\ m^\mu &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}(r+ia\cos\theta)}(ia\sin\theta, 0, 1, i\csc\theta) \end{align}

Weyl scalars: \(\Psi_0 = -C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} l^\mu m^\nu l^\rho m^\sigma\), \(\Psi_4 = -C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} n^\mu \bar{m}^\nu n^\rho \bar{m}^\sigma\).

Maxwell scalars: \(\Phi_0 = F_{\mu\nu} l^\mu m^\nu\), \(\Phi_2 = F_{\mu\nu} n^\mu \bar{m}^\nu\).

4.2 Teukolsky Master Equation

For a field of spin weight \(s\) (\(s=0\): scalar, \(s=\pm 1\): EM, \(s=\pm 2\): gravitational):

\begin{equation} \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &\left[ \frac{(r^2+a^2)^2}{\Delta} - a^2\sin^2\theta \right] \partial_t^2 \Psi + \frac{4Mar}{\Delta} \partial_t \partial_\phi \Psi + \left[ \frac{a^2}{\Delta} - \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta} \right] \partial_\phi^2 \Psi \\ &- \Delta^{-s} \partial_r (\Delta^{s+1} \partial_r \Psi) - \frac{1}{\sin\theta} \partial_\theta (\sin\theta \partial_\theta \Psi) \\ &- 2s \left[ \frac{a(r-M)}{\Delta} + \frac{i\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta} \right] \partial_\phi \Psi + 2s \left[ \frac{M(r^2-a^2)}{\Delta} - r - ia\cos\theta \right] \partial_t \Psi \\ &+ (s^2\cot^2\theta - s)\Psi = 0 \end{aligned} } \end{equation}

For \(s=0\): scalar field. For \(s=\pm 1\): \(\Phi_0\) (\(s=1\)) and \(\Phi_2\) (\(s=-1\)). For \(s=\pm 2\): \(\Psi_0\) (\(s=2\)) and \(\Psi_4\) (\(s=-2\)).

4.3 Separation of Variables

Assume \(\Psi = e^{-i\omega t + im\phi} S_{s\ell m}(\theta) R_{s\ell m}(r)\).

#### Angular Equation \begin{equation} \frac{1}{\sin\theta} \frac{d}{d\theta} \left( \sin\theta \frac{dS}{d\theta} \right) + \left[ a^2\omega^2\cos^2\theta - \frac{(m+s\cos\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta} + 2a\omega s\cos\theta + {}_s\lambda_{\ell m} \right] S = 0 \end{equation} Solutions: spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics \({}_sS_{\ell m}(\theta; a\omega)\).

#### Radial Equation \begin{equation} \Delta^{-s} \frac{d}{dr} \left( \Delta^{s+1} \frac{dR}{dr} \right) + \left[ \frac{K^2 - 2is(r-M)K}{\Delta} + 4is\omega r - {}_s\lambda_{\ell m} \right] R = 0 \end{equation} where \(K = (r^2+a^2)\omega - am\).

5. Quasi-Normal Modes (QNMs)

5.1 Boundary Conditions

QNMs are solutions to the master equation with:

  • Ingoing at horizon: \(\Psi \sim e^{-i\omega r_}\) as \(r_ \to -\infty\)
  • Outgoing at infinity: \(\Psi \sim e^{+i\omega r_}\) as \(r_ \to +\infty\)

5.2 QNM Frequency Spectrum

The frequencies \(\omega_n\) are complex: \(\omega_n = \omega_R - i\omega_I\) with \(\omega_I > 0\) (damping).

For Schwarzschild (\(a=0\)), \(\ell=2\) gravitational modes (\(s=-2\)): \begin{align} \omega_0 M &= 0.37367 - 0.08896i \\ \omega_1 M &= 0.34671 - 0.27391i \\ \omega_2 M &= 0.30106 - 0.47828i \\ &\vdots \end{align}

For Kerr (\(a \neq 0\)), frequencies depend on \(a/M\) and \(m\). Prograde modes (\(m>0\)) have higher frequency; retrograde modes have lower.

5.3 QNM Excitation and Ringdown

A perturbed black hole rings down as a superposition of QNMs: \begin{equation} \Psi(t,r_) = \sum_n A_n e^{-i\omega_n t} \psi_n(r_) + \text{branch cut (late-time tail)} \end{equation}

The ringdown signal from a binary merger is dominated by the fundamental \(\ell=2\) mode.

6. Green's Functions

6.1 Retarded Green's Function

The retarded Green's function satisfies: \begin{equation} \left[ \frac{d^2}{dr_^2} + \omega^2 - V(r) \right] G_\omega(r_, r_') = \delta(r_ - r_*') \end{equation}

With boundary conditions: \(G \sim e^{i\omega r_}\) as \(r_ \to +\infty\), \(G \sim e^{-i\omega r_}\) as \(r_ \to -\infty\).

6.2 Construction from Homogeneous Solutions

Let \(\psi_{\text{in}}(r_)\) satisfy ingoing BC at horizon, \(\psi_{\text{up}}(r_)\) satisfy outgoing BC at infinity.

Wronskian: \(W = \psi_{\text{in}} \psi_{\text{up}}' - \psi_{\text{in}}' \psi_{\text{up}} = \text{const}\).

Then: \begin{equation} G_\omega(r_, r_') = \frac{1}{W} \begin{cases} \psi_{\text{in}}(r_) \psi_{\text{up}}(r_') & r_ < r_' \\ \psi_{\text{up}}(r_) \psi_{\text{in}}(r_') & r_ > r_' \end{cases} \end{equation}

6.3 Time-Domain Green's Function

Inverse Fourier transform: \begin{equation} G(t, r_, r_') = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^\infty G_\omega(r_, r_') e^{-i\omega t} d\omega \end{equation}

Contour integration picks up QNM poles and branch cut: \begin{equation} G(t, r_, r_') = \sum_n \text{Res}(\omega_n) e^{-i\omega_n t} \psi_n(r_) \psi_n(r_') + G_{\text{branch}}(t, r_, r_') \end{equation}

6.4 Late-Time Tails

At late times, the branch cut dominates: \begin{equation} \Psi(t, r_*) \sim t^{-(2\ell+3)} \quad \text{(Schwarzschild, Price's law)} \end{equation} For Kerr, the tail is more complicated due to superradiance and ergoregion.

7. Superradiance

7.1 Superradiant Condition

For a wave with frequency \(\omega\) and azimuthal number \(m\) incident on a Kerr black hole:

\begin{equation} \boxed{0 < \omega < m \Omega_H} \end{equation}

where \(\Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2)\) is the horizon angular velocity.

In this regime, the reflection coefficient \(|R|^2 > 1\): the wave is amplified.

7.2 Energy Extraction

The amplified wave extracts rotational energy from the black hole. The rate of energy extraction:

\begin{equation} \frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^\infty d\omega \sum_{\ell m} (\omega - m\Omega_H) |\mathcal{A}_{\ell m}(\omega)|^2 \end{equation}

where \(\mathcal{A}\) is the absorption amplitude.

7.3 Black Hole Bomb

If a mirror surrounds the black hole (or massive field provides effective mirror), superradiance leads to exponential growth (instability). For massive scalar field \(\mu\): \begin{equation} \omega \approx \mu - \frac{\mu^3 M^2}{2(\ell+1)^2} + i \Gamma \end{equation} Growth rate \(\Gamma > 0\) for \(\mu M \ll 1\).

8. Gravitational Wave Generation: Teukolsky-Sasaki-Nakamura

8.1 Source Term

For a point particle of mass \(\mu\) on a geodesic, the stress-energy tensor: \begin{equation} T^{\mu\nu} = \mu \int \frac{u^\mu u^\nu}{\sqrt{-g}} \delta^4(x - x_p(\tau)) d\tau \end{equation}

The Teukolsky equation with source: \begin{equation} \mathcal{O}_s \Psi = T_s \end{equation} where \(T_s\) is a projection of \(T_{\mu\nu}\) onto the NP tetrad.

8.2 Sasaki-Nakamura Transformation

The Teukolsky radial equation has a short-range potential that is numerically inconvenient. The Sasaki-Nakamura (SN) equation has a short-range potential and is better suited for numerical integration.

Transformation: \(X = \mathcal{D} R\) where \(\mathcal{D}\) is a differential operator.

SN equation: \begin{equation} \frac{d^2 X}{dr_^2} - F(r) \frac{dX}{dr_} - U(r) X = 0 \end{equation} with \(F(r) \to 0\), \(U(r) \to \omega^2\) as \(r \to \infty\), and \(U(r) \to \omega^2 - 2i\omega \kappa\) as \(r \to r_+\).

8.3 Waveform at Infinity

The gravitational waveform \(h_+ - i h_\times\) is related to \(\Psi_4\): \begin{equation} \Psi_4 = \frac{1}{2} (\ddot{h}_+ - i \ddot{h}_\times) \end{equation}

At large \(r\): \begin{equation} \Psi_4 \sim \frac{1}{r} \sum_{\ell m} Z_{\ell m} e^{-i\omega t} {}_sS_{\ell m}(\theta) \end{equation}

The energy flux: \begin{equation} \frac{dE}{dt} = \sum_{\ell m} \frac{|Z_{\ell m}|^2}{4\pi \omega^2} \end{equation}

9. Self-Force and Radiation Reaction

9.1 Self-Force Problem

A point particle of mass \(\mu\) moving in a background black hole creates a metric perturbation \(h_{\mu\nu}\). The particle's motion deviates from a geodesic due to its own field.

The equation of motion: \begin{equation} \mu \frac{D u^\mu}{d\tau} = F^\mu_{\text{self}} \end{equation}

9.2 Regularization

The self-force diverges at the particle's location. Regularization schemes:

  • Mode-sum regularization: Decompose into \(\ell\)-modes, subtract singular part.
  • Effective source: Replace delta function with smooth effective source.
  • Puncture method: Split field into singular (analytic) and regular (numerical) parts.

9.3 First-Order Self-Force

For a particle in Schwarzschild: \begin{equation} F^r_{\text{self}} = \mu^2 \left[ -\frac{1}{3} \frac{M}{r^3} + \cdots \right] \quad \text{(conservative)} \end{equation} \begin{equation} F^t_{\text{self}} = \mu^2 \left[ \frac{32}{5} \frac{M^3}{r^5} \dot{r} + \cdots \right] \quad \text{(dissipative)} \end{equation}

10. Green's Functions in Kerr: MST Method

10.1 Mano-Suzuki-Takasugi (MST) Method

Analytic solution of the Teukolsky radial equation using:

  • Hypergeometric functions near horizon
  • Coulomb wave functions at infinity
  • Series of hypergeometric functions (convergent everywhere)

10.2 Renormalized Angular Momentum

Introduce \(\nu\) such that the angular eigenvalue \({}_s\lambda_{\ell m}\) is expressed as a continued fraction in \(\nu\). The parameter \(\nu\) is determined by a continued fraction equation.

10.3 Connection Formulae

The MST method provides explicit connection coefficients between horizon and infinity solutions, enabling high-precision QNM calculations and waveform generation.

11. Black Hole Spectroscopy

11.1 No-Hair Theorem Tests

QNM frequencies depend only on \(M\) and \(a\) (and \(Q\) for KN). Measuring multiple QNMs tests the Kerr hypothesis.

11.2 Ringdown Overtones

The ringdown signal: \begin{equation} h(t) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} A_n e^{-i\omega_n t} + \text{noise} \end{equation}

Bayesian inference on \(A_n, \omega_n\) constrains \(M, a\).

11.3 Parametrized Ringdown

Introduce deviations \(\delta \omega_n / \omega_n^{\text{Kerr}}\) to test alternative theories.

12. Perturbations of Reissner-Nordström and Kerr-Newman

12.1 Coupled Gravitational-EM Perturbations

For RN, gravitational and electromagnetic perturbations couple. The master equations are a system of two coupled ODEs.

12.2 Moncrief's Formalism

Define gauge-invariant master variables for coupled system. The equations decouple into two independent master equations.

12.3 Kerr-Newman: Separation of Variables

The Teukolsky equation for KN includes charge \(Q\). The separation works identically to Kerr, with \(\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 + Q^2\).

13. Numerical Methods

13.1 Frequency Domain

  • Direct integration: Integrate from horizon and infinity, match at a point.
  • Shooting method: Vary \(\omega\) to satisfy boundary conditions.
  • MST method: Analytic continuation with hypergeometric series.
  • Leaver's continued fraction: For QNMs, express solution as series with continued fraction condition.

13.2 Time Domain

  • Characteristic evolution: Integrate 1+1D wave equation on \(r_*\)-\(t\) grid.
  • Finite difference: Discretize \(\partial_t^2 - \partial_{r_*}^2 + V\).
  • Pseudospectral: Expand in Chebyshev polynomials.

13.3 Self-Force Codes

  • Lorenz gauge: Solve \(\Box \bar{h}_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}\) with \(\nabla^\mu \bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=0\).
  • Radiation gauge: Use Teukolsky + Hertz potential reconstruction.

14. Effective One Body (EOB) and Phenomenological Models

14.1 EOB Formalism

Maps two-body problem to a test particle in a deformed Schwarzschild/Kerr metric.

Hamiltonian: \begin{equation} H_{\text{EOB}} = M \sqrt{1 + 2\nu \left( \frac{H_{\text{eff}}}{\mu} - 1 \right)} \end{equation}

Radiation reaction from resummed PN flux + QNM ringdown.

14.2 Phenomenological Waveforms (IMRPhenom)

Fit numerical relativity waveforms with analytic expressions: \begin{equation} h(f) = A(f) e^{i\psi(f)} \end{equation} where \(A(f)\) and \(\psi(f)\) are calibrated to NR simulations.

15. Gauge Invariance and Master Variables

15.1 Gauge Transformations

Under \(x^\mu \to x^\mu + \xi^\mu\): \begin{equation} h_{\mu\nu} \to h_{\mu\nu} - \nabla_\mu \xi_\nu - \nabla_\nu \xi_\mu \end{equation}

15.2 Gauge-Invariant Variables (Schwarzschild)

For axial: \(k_1 = h_1 - \frac{r}{2} \partial_r h_0 + \frac{1}{2r} h_0\) (Regge-Wheeler). For polar: Zerilli variable \(\Psi_Z\).

For Kerr: The Teukolsky scalars \(\Psi_0\) and \(\Psi_4\) are gauge-invariant (for \(s=\pm 2\)).

15.3 Hertz Potential Reconstruction

From \(\Psi_0\) or \(\Psi_4\), reconstruct the metric perturbation via a Hertz potential \(\Psi_H\): \begin{equation} h_{\mu\nu} = \mathcal{O}_{\mu\nu} \Psi_H \end{equation}

18. Appendices

18.1 Derivation of Regge-Wheeler Equation

Start with linearized Einstein equations \(\delta G_{\mu\nu} = 0\) for axial perturbations.

The \((t,\theta)\) and \((t,\phi)\) components give: \begin{align} \partial_t h_1 - \partial_r h_0 + \frac{2}{r} h_0 &= 0 \\ \partial_t \partial_r \left( \frac{h_0}{r} \right) - \frac{f}{r} \partial_r (r h_1) + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} f h_0 &= 0 \end{align}

Define \(\Psi = \frac{r}{\lambda} (h_1 - \frac{r}{2} \partial_r h_0)\). Eliminate \(h_0, h_1\) to get the master equation.

18.2 Derivation of Teukolsky Equation

Start with the Bianchi identities and Maxwell equations in NP formalism: \begin{align} (D - \rho - \bar{\rho} + 3\varepsilon - \bar{\varepsilon}) \Psi_1 - (\delta - \tau + 3\beta - \bar{\alpha}) \Psi_0 &= \text{source} \\ (\Delta + \mu - 3\gamma + \bar{\gamma}) \Psi_0 - (\delta + 2\beta - \tau) \Psi_1 &= \text{source} \end{align}

Eliminate \(\Psi_1\) using the commutator relations. The result is the Teukolsky equation for \(\Psi_0\) (spin \(s=2\)).

18.3 Sasaki-Nakamura Transformation

For \(s=-2\) (ingoing radiation), define: \begin{equation} X = \frac{\Delta^2}{r^4} \left[ \frac{r^4}{\Delta^2} \left( \alpha R + \beta \frac{dR}{dr} \right) \right] \end{equation} where \(\alpha, \beta\) are chosen so that \(X\) satisfies a short-range potential equation.
