Early supermassive black-hole growth

How do billion-solar-mass black holes exist less than a billion years after the Big Bang? This page derives the Eddington-limited growth model and the constraints it places on seeds.

1. Eddington-limited accretion

For fully ionized hydrogen, radiation pressure balances gravity when

$$ L_{\rm Edd} = \frac{4\pi G M m_{p} c}{\sigma_{T}} \approx 1.26\times10^{38}\,\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\ {\rm erg/s} $$

With radiative efficiency ε = L/(Ṁin c²) and duty cycle f, the retained mass growth rate is

$$ \frac{dM}{dt} = \frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,f\,\lambda\,\frac{4\pi G m_{p}}{\sigma_{T} c}\,M $$

where λ = L/LEdd. For constant parameters this integrates to exponential growth M(t) = M0 exp(t/tef) with e-folding time

$$ t_{\rm ef} = \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,\frac{\sigma_{T} c}{4\pi G m_{p}}\,\frac{1}{f\lambda} $$

For ε = 0.1, f = λ = 1: tef ≈ 50 Myr. Pure hydrogen, Thomson opacity.

2. The seed constraint

Given an observed mass Mobs at redshift zo and a seed at zf, the required seed is

$$ M_{\rm seed,req} = M_{\rm obs}\,\exp\!\left[-\frac{\Delta t}{t_{\rm ef}}\right] $$

with Δt = t(zo) − t(zf) from a ΛCDM cosmology (here Planck 2018). The notebook samples ε ∈ [0.057, 0.2], f ∈ [0.3, 1], λ ∈ [0.3, 3] and shows that a 109 M⊙ hole at z ≈ 7.5 can be grown from a ~102–105 M⊙ seed depending on the duty cycle and Eddington ratio — i.e. no single seed channel is uniquely required.

3. The Soltan argument

The total mass density in black holes today must equal the integrated accreted mass: ρBH(z=0) = (1−ε) ∫ (dρacc/dt) dt. This links the observed quasar luminosity function to the local black-hole mass function and constrains the average radiative efficiency to ε ≈ 0.1.

4. Local anchor: reverberation-mapped masses

The 86-source AGN reverberation-mapped catalog (real data) provides the local mass distribution: median log M ≈ 7.49, spanning ~4.4 dex. These are local (z < 1) masses and serve as a method-comparison anchor, not as an early-universe sample.

5. Assumptions and caveats

  • Constant ε, f, λ over the growth history — real accretion is episodic.
  • Thomson opacity for pure H; heavier elements lower LEdd.
  • Mergers are not modelled; they would reduce the required seed.
  • High-redshift masses are single-epoch virial or Eddington-inferred, not dynamical — method-dependent systematics of ~0.3–0.5 dex.
  • No population abundance inference is attempted; the sample is luminosity-selected.

6. References

  • Soltan A., 1982, MNRAS 200, 115 — the Soltan argument.
  • Shapiro S. L., 2005, ApJ 620, 59 — BH growth and feedback.
  • Inayoshi K., Visbal E., Haiman Z., 2020, ARA&A 58, 27 — early SMBH formation.
