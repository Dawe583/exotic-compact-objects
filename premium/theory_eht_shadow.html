Black-hole shadows and the EHT measurements

The Event Horizon Telescope images of M87* and Sgr A* are not pictures of the hole itself but of the photon ring around it. The size of that ring is a clean test of the Kerr metric in the strong-field regime.

1. Why a black hole casts a shadow

Photons that pass too close to a black hole are captured; those that pass slightly farther away are strongly deflected and can orbit. The boundary between capture and escape defines a critical impact parameter bcrit, and the set of directions on the sky from which photons reach the observer forms a dark disk — the shadow. Its angular size is determined entirely by the mass, spin and distance of the hole; the plasma physics only affects brightness, not the outline.

2. Schwarzschild shadow (exact)

For a non-spinning hole the critical impact parameter is

$$ b_{\rm crit} = 3\sqrt{3}\,\frac{GM}{c^{2}} \approx 5.196\,\frac{GM}{c^{2}} $$

The shadow is a disk of angular radius bcrit/DL, so its angular diameter is

$$ \theta_{\rm sh} = 2 b_{\rm crit}/D_{L} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}\,GM}{c^{2} D_{L}} $$

This is a pure strong-field prediction: no free parameters once M and DL are measured independently.

3. Kerr correction

Spin breaks spherical symmetry. The shadow becomes slightly non-circular and its size depends on the inclination i and spin a:

  • Prograde (observer on the spin axis side) the shadow shrinks by a few percent for a = 0.9.
  • Retrograde it grows by a comparable amount.
  • The shape acquires a horizontal offset (the "crescent") of order a/M.

A convenient first-order approximation used below is θKerr ≈ θSchw (1 − 0.05 a) for a prograde view, accurate to ~1% for a ≲ 0.9.

The EHT "ring" is a bright photon ring, slightly larger than the shadow edge, so a ratio of ~1.0–1.1 is expected. Both sources are consistent with Kerr at the ~5% level — a genuine strong-field test.

5. What this does and does not test

  • Tests: the relation M–D–θ in the strong field; the existence of an event horizon (no surface); spin magnitude and orientation.
  • Does not test: the detailed spacetime multipole structure (needs higher-resolution imaging); alternative theories that predict the same shadow size at leading order.
  • Caveats: the mass of M87* comes from stellar dynamics (Gebhardt et al. 2011) and is independent of the EHT image; the distance is from the M87* jet (16.8 Mpc). For Sgr A* the mass and distance come from stellar orbits (GRAVITY). The ring diameter is a model-dependent extraction from the visibilities, with ~10% systematic uncertainty.

6. Assumptions

  • Asymptotically flat Kerr metric; the hole is isolated (no significant accretion-disk back-reaction on the metric).
  • Observer at infinity; the shadow edge is well approximated by the critical impact parameter even for rotating holes (Bardeen 1973).
  • The EHT ring diameter is taken as the reported value; no re-fitting of the visibilities is performed here.
  • All quantities are derived from public EHT summary parameters, not from a full GRMHD fit.
