The Penrose process: extracting energy from a rotating black hole

A rotating black hole stores rotational energy in its ergosphere. The Penrose process shows how to tap it: split a particle inside the ergosphere so that one fragment falls in with negative energy and the other escapes with more energy than the original.

1. The ergosphere

In the Kerr metric the static limit (where gtt = 0) lies outside the horizon for a ≠ 0:

$$ r_{\rm SL}(\theta) = M + \sqrt{M^{2} - a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta} $$

(in units G = c = 1). Between r+ (horizon) and rSL is the ergosphere: a region where no observer can remain static, and where particle energies can be negative.

2. The process

A particle of energy E0 enters the ergosphere and decays into two fragments:

  • Fragment 1 (energy E1 > 0) escapes to infinity.
  • Fragment 2 (energy E2 < 0) falls into the hole.

Energy conservation: E0 = E1 + E2, so E1 = E0 − E2 > E0. The black hole loses mass-energy |E2| and angular momentum; repeated extraction spins it down.

3. Efficiency

The efficiency of a single step is

$$ \eta = \frac{E_{1} - E_{0}}{E_{0}} = -\frac{E_{2}}{E_{0}} $$

For an extremal Kerr hole (a = M) the maximum single-step efficiency is

$$ \eta_{\rm max} \approx 20.7\% $$

With repeated (iterative) extraction the total efficiency can approach ~29% (the total rotational energy fraction of an extremal hole). By comparison, nuclear fusion releases ~0.7% of rest mass.

4. Conditions for negative energy

A particle has negative energy only if its angular momentum L satisfies L < (E/ΩH) in the ergosphere, where ΩH = a/(2 M r+) is the horizon angular velocity. The fragment must be counter-rotating relative to the hole's spin.

5. Astrophysical relevance

  • The Blandford-Znajek process (magnetic extraction) is the magnetic analogue and is thought to power relativistic jets in AGN and GRBs; its efficiency can exceed the Penrose value.
  • Superradiant scattering of waves is the wave analogue: a wave scattered off a rotating hole can be amplified (Zel'dovich/Starobinsky).
  • The Penrose process itself is not observed directly; it is a proof of principle that rotational energy is extractable.

6. Assumptions and caveats

  • Test-particle approximation: the fragments are assumed not to back-react on the metric.
  • The decay must occur inside the ergosphere; outside, E2 cannot be negative.
  • Extremal spin (a = M) is an idealisation; real holes are expected to spin below the Thorne limit a ≈ 0.998.
  • All efficiencies are theoretical upper bounds; real extraction is limited by plasma physics and magnetic fields.

7. References

  • Penrose R., 1969, Riv. Nuovo Cimento 1, 252 — original process.
  • Bardeen J. M., Press W. H., Teukolsky S. A., 1972, ApJ 178, 347 — Kerr geodesics and extraction.
  • Blandford R. D., Znajek R. L., 1977, MNRAS 179, 433 — magnetic extraction.
