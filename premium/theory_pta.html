Pulsar timing arrays and gravitational-wave detection

A pulsar timing array (PTA) uses millisecond pulsars as a galaxy-scale gravitational-wave detector. This page derives the timing residual, the single-pulsar SNR, and shows which real pulsars are the best targets.

1. The signal: a timing residual

A gravitational wave of strain h and frequency f propagating through the Earth-pulsar baseline modulates the arrival times of pulses. The induced timing residual has amplitude

$$ \delta t \;\sim\; \frac{h}{2\pi f} $$

For a fiducial h = 10-15 at f = 1 nHz (the PTA band), δt ≈ 160 ns — comparable to the best timing precisions (~100 ns).

2. Single-pulsar SNR

For a pulsar timed with precision σt over a span Tobs with cadence Δt, the SNR is approximately

$$ {\rm SNR} \;\approx\; \frac{\delta t}{\sigma_{t}}\sqrt{\frac{T_{\rm obs}}{\Delta t}} $$

The figure of merit is therefore δt/σt: closer pulsars (larger geometric factor) and brighter pulsars (smaller σt) are preferred.

3. The Hellings-Downs curve

For a gravitational-wave background, the correlation between the timing residuals of two pulsars depends only on their angular separation ζ on the sky:

$$ \Gamma(\zeta) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}\frac{1-\cos\zeta}{2} + \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1-\cos\zeta}{2}\right)\ln\left(\frac{1-\cos\zeta}{2}\right) $$

This quadrupolar pattern is the smoking gun of a gravitational-wave origin (as opposed to clock or ephemeris errors, which give monopolar/dipolar correlations). The 2023 NANOGrav/PPTE/EPTA/CPTA detections all report a Hellings-Downs correlation.

5. Assumptions and caveats

  • The timing precision σt is not in the ATNF catalog; the SNR proxy uses a flux-based scaling and is indicative only.
  • The residual formula ignores the geometric (antenna) factor, which varies from 0 to ~0.5 depending on the GW propagation direction relative to the pulsar.
  • The stochastic background is assumed isotropic; anisotropies or individual supermassive binaries would change the correlation pattern.
  • Real PTA analyses use a full Bayesian likelihood over all pulsars jointly, not single-pulsar SNRs.
