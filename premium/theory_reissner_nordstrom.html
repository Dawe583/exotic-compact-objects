Reissner-Nordström Black Hole: Complete Theoretical Derivation

1. Einstein-Maxwell Action and Field Equations

The Reissner-Nordström solution describes a static, spherically symmetric black hole with mass \(M\) and electric charge \(Q\). We begin with the Einstein-Maxwell action:

\begin{equation} S = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{1}{16\pi G} R - \frac{1}{4} F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu} \right] \end{equation}

where \(F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu\) is the electromagnetic field tensor. Varying with respect to \(g^{\mu\nu}\) and \(A_\mu\) yields the coupled field equations:

\begin{align} G_{\mu\nu} &= 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{(EM)}} \\ \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} &= 0 \end{align}

with the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor:

\begin{equation} T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{(EM)}} = \frac{1}{4\pi} \left( F_{\mu\alpha} F_\nu^{\;\alpha} - \frac{1}{4} g_{\mu\nu} F_{\alpha\beta} F^{\alpha\beta} \right) \end{equation}

2. Spherically Symmetric Ansatz

For a static, spherically symmetric spacetime, the metric takes the form:

\begin{equation} ds^2 = -f(r) dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f(r)} + r^2 d\Omega^2 \end{equation}

where \(d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta d\phi^2\). The electromagnetic potential for a purely electric charge is:

\begin{equation} A_\mu dx^\mu = -\Phi(r) dt, \quad \Phi(r) = \frac{Q}{r} \end{equation}

The non-zero components of \(F_{\mu\nu}\) are:

\begin{equation} F_{tr} = -F_{rt} = \partial_r \Phi = -\frac{Q}{r^2} \end{equation}

The invariant \(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\) evaluates to:

\begin{equation} F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = 2 F_{tr}F^{tr} = -\frac{2Q^2}{r^4} \end{equation}

3. Einstein Equations for the Metric Function

The non-zero components of the Einstein tensor for the ansatz (4) are:

\begin{align} G_{tt} &= \frac{f}{r^2} \frac{d}{dr}\left[ r(1-f) \right] \\ G_{rr} &= -\frac{1}{r^2 f} \frac{d}{dr}\left[ r(1-f) \right] \\ G_{\theta\theta} &= \frac{r^2}{2} f'' + r f' \\ G_{\phi\phi} &= \sin^2\theta \, G_{\theta\theta} \end{align}

The electromagnetic stress-energy components are:

\begin{align} T_{tt}^{\text{(EM)}} &= \frac{Q^2}{8\pi r^4} f \\ T_{rr}^{\text{(EM)}} &= -\frac{Q^2}{8\pi r^4 f} \\ T_{\theta\theta}^{\text{(EM)}} &= -\frac{Q^2}{8\pi r^2} \\ T_{\phi\phi}^{\text{(EM)}} &= \sin^2\theta \, T_{\theta\theta}^{\text{(EM)}} \end{align}

The \(tt\) (or \(rr\)) Einstein equation gives:

\begin{equation} \frac{1}{r^2} \frac{d}{dr}\left[ r(1-f) \right] = \frac{G Q^2}{r^4} \end{equation}

Integrating once:

\begin{equation} r(1-f) = 2GM - \frac{G Q^2}{r} \end{equation}

Solving for \(f(r)\):

\begin{equation} \boxed{f(r) = 1 - \frac{2GM}{r} + \frac{G Q^2}{r^2}} \end{equation}

This is the Reissner-Nordström metric function.

4. Horizon Structure

Horizons occur where \(f(r) = 0\):

\begin{equation} r^2 - 2GM r + G Q^2 = 0 \end{equation}

The roots are:

\begin{equation} r_\pm = GM \pm \sqrt{G^2 M^2 - G Q^2} \end{equation}

\begin{itemize} \item \(r_+\): Event horizon (outer horizon) \item \(r_-\): Cauchy horizon (inner horizon) \end{itemize}

4.1 Three Regimes

\begin{align} \textbf{Subextremal:} &\quad G M^2 > Q^2 \quad (r_+ > r_-) \\ \textbf{Extremal:} &\quad G M^2 = Q^2 \quad (r_+ = r_- = GM) \\ \textbf{Superextremal:} &\quad G M^2 < Q^2 \quad (\text{naked singularity, no horizon}) \end{align}

The cosmic censorship conjecture forbids the superextremal case.

5. Extremal Limit and Near-Horizon Geometry

In the extremal limit \(Q^2 = G M^2\), the two horizons coincide:

\begin{equation} r_+ = r_- = r_e = GM \end{equation}

Define near-horizon coordinates:

\begin{equation} r = r_e + \lambda \rho, \quad t = \frac{\tau}{\lambda}, \quad \lambda \to 0 \end{equation}

The metric becomes:

\begin{equation} ds^2 = \frac{\rho^2}{r_e^2} d\tau^2 - \frac{r_e^2}{\rho^2} d\rho^2 - r_e^2 d\Omega^2 \end{equation}

This is \(\text{AdS}_2 \times S^2\) with radii \(r_e\) for both factors.

6. Surface Gravity and Hawking Temperature

The surface gravity is defined by:

\begin{equation} \kappa = \frac{1}{2} \left. \frac{df}{dr} \right|_{r=r_+} \end{equation}

Computing:

\begin{equation} \frac{df}{dr} = \frac{2GM}{r^2} - \frac{2GQ^2}{r^3} \end{equation}

Evaluating at \(r_+\):

\begin{equation} \kappa = \frac{GM}{r_+^2} - \frac{GQ^2}{r_+^3} = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2r_+^2} \end{equation}

Using \(r_+ r_- = G Q^2\) and \(r_+ + r_- = 2GM\):

\begin{equation} \boxed{\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{G^2 M^2 - G Q^2}}{r_+^2} = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2r_+^2}} \end{equation}

The Hawking temperature is:

\begin{equation} \boxed{T_H = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{4\pi r_+^2} = \frac{\sqrt{G^2 M^2 - G Q^2}}{2\pi r_+^2}} \end{equation}

7. Black Hole Entropy

The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is one-quarter the horizon area in Planck units:

\begin{equation} S = \frac{A_+}{4G} = \frac{4\pi r_+^2}{4G} = \frac{\pi r_+^2}{G} \end{equation}

In terms of \(M\) and \(Q\):

\begin{equation} \boxed{S = \pi \left( GM + \sqrt{G^2 M^2 - G Q^2} \right)^2 / G} \end{equation}

8. Thermodynamics: First Law and Smarr Formula

The first law of black hole mechanics for RN black holes:

\begin{equation} \boxed{dM = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi G} dA + \Phi_+ dQ} \end{equation}

where \(\Phi_+ = Q/r_+\) is the electric potential at the horizon.

\begin{proof} Vary \(M = \frac{r_+}{2G} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+}\) (from \(f(r_+)=0\)):

\begin{align} dM &= \frac{dr_+}{2G} + \frac{Q}{r_+} dQ - \frac{Q^2}{2r_+^2} dr_+ \\ &= \left( \frac{1}{2G} - \frac{Q^2}{2r_+^2} \right) dr_+ + \frac{Q}{r_+} dQ \\ &= \frac{r_+^2 - G Q^2}{2G r_+^2} dr_+ + \Phi_+ dQ \\ &= \frac{r_+ - r_-}{4G r_+^2} (8\pi r_+ dr_+) + \Phi_+ dQ \\ &= \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{dA}{8\pi G} + \Phi_+ dQ \quad \text{(using } dA = 8\pi r_+ dr_+) \\ &= T_H dS + \Phi_+ dQ \end{align} \end{proof}

The Smarr formula (from scaling arguments):

\begin{equation} \boxed{M = \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi G} + \Phi_+ Q = 2 T_H S + \Phi_+ Q} \end{equation}

9. Thermodynamic Potentials

9.1 Helmholtz Free Energy (Canonical Ensemble, Fixed \(Q\))

\begin{equation} F(T, Q) = M - T_H S \end{equation}

Using \(M = \frac{r_+}{2G} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+}\) and \(T_H = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{4\pi r_+^2}\), \(S = \frac{\pi r_+^2}{G}\):

\begin{align} F &= \frac{r_+}{2G} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+} - \frac{r_+ - r_-}{4\pi r_+^2} \cdot \frac{\pi r_+^2}{G} \\ &= \frac{r_+}{2G} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+} - \frac{r_+ - r_-}{4G} \\ &= \frac{r_+ + r_-}{4G} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+} \\ &= \frac{GM}{2G} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+} = \frac{M}{2} + \frac{Q^2}{2r_+} \end{align}

9.2 Gibbs Free Energy (Grand Canonical Ensemble, Fixed \(\Phi\))

\begin{equation} G(T, \Phi) = M - T_H S - \Phi Q \end{equation}

Using \(\Phi = Q/r_+\) and the Smarr formula:

\begin{equation} G = M - \left( \frac{M}{2} - \frac{\Phi Q}{2} \right) - \Phi Q = \frac{M}{2} - \frac{\Phi Q}{2} \end{equation}

10. Heat Capacity and Phase Transitions

The heat capacity at constant charge:

\begin{equation} C_Q = T_H \left( \frac{\partial S}{\partial T_H} \right)_Q \end{equation}

Compute \(\partial S / \partial r_+ = 2\pi r_+/G\) and \(\partial T_H / \partial r_+\):

\begin{equation} T_H = \frac{1}{4\pi} \left( \frac{1}{r_+} - \frac{G Q^2}{r_+^3} \right) \end{equation}

\begin{equation} \frac{\partial T_H}{\partial r_+} = \frac{1}{4\pi} \left( -\frac{1}{r_+^2} + \frac{3G Q^2}{r_+^4} \right) = \frac{3G Q^2 - r_+^2}{4\pi r_+^4} \end{equation}

Thus:

\begin{equation} C_Q = T_H \frac{2\pi r_+/G}{(3G Q^2 - r_+^2)/(4\pi r_+^4)} = \frac{2\pi r_+^2}{G} \frac{r_+^2 - G Q^2}{3G Q^2 - r_+^2} \end{equation}

Using \(r_+^2 - G Q^2 = r_+(r_+ - r_-)\):

\begin{equation} \boxed{C_Q = \frac{2\pi r_+^2}{G} \frac{r_+(r_+ - r_-)}{3G Q^2 - r_+^2}} \end{equation}

\(C_Q\) diverges at \(r_+^2 = 3G Q^2\), signaling a Davies phase transition. For \(r_+^2 < 3G Q^2\), \(C_Q < 0\) (thermodynamically unstable); for \(r_+^2 > 3G Q^2\), \(C_Q > 0\) (stable).

11. Schwinger Pair Production and Black Hole Discharge

A charged black hole can discharge via Schwinger pair production in its electric field. The electric field near the horizon:

\begin{equation} E = \frac{Q}{r_+^2} \end{equation}

The Schwinger pair production rate per unit volume for electrons/positrons:

\begin{equation} \Gamma = \frac{(eE)^2}{4\pi^3} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \exp\left( -\frac{n\pi m_e^2}{eE} \right) \end{equation}

For \(eE \ll m_e^2\), the leading term dominates:

\begin{equation} \Gamma \approx \frac{e^2 E^2}{4\pi^3} \exp\left( -\frac{\pi m_e^2}{eE} \right) \end{equation}

The discharge rate:

\begin{equation} \frac{dQ}{dt} = -e \int \Gamma \, dV \end{equation}

Near the horizon, integrate over a shell of thickness \(\sim 1/E\):

\begin{equation} \frac{dQ}{dt} \sim -e \cdot \frac{e^2 E^2}{4\pi^3} \cdot 4\pi r_+^2 \cdot \frac{1}{E} \exp\left( -\frac{\pi m_e^2}{eE} \right) \end{equation}

\begin{equation} \frac{dQ}{dt} \sim -\frac{e^3 E r_+^2}{\pi^2} \exp\left( -\frac{\pi m_e^2 r_+^2}{e Q} \right) \end{equation}

Substituting \(E = Q/r_+^2\):

\begin{equation} \boxed{\frac{dQ}{dt} \sim -\frac{e^3 Q}{\pi^2 r_+^2} \exp\left( -\frac{\pi m_e^2 r_+^2}{e Q} \right)} \end{equation}

12. Discharge Timescale

For a near-extremal black hole, \(r_+ \approx GM \approx Q/\sqrt{G}\). The exponent becomes:

\begin{equation} \frac{\pi m_e^2 r_+^2}{e Q} \approx \frac{\pi m_e^2 Q}{e \sqrt{G}} = \pi \frac{m_e^2}{e} \frac{Q}{\sqrt{G}} \end{equation}

In Planck units (\(G = 1\)), for a solar mass black hole \(M \sim 10^{38}\), \(Q \sim 10^{38}\):

\begin{equation} \frac{\pi m_e^2 Q}{e} \sim \pi \times (10^{-22})^2 \times 10^{38} / 10^{-1} \sim 10^{-6} \ll 1 \end{equation}

The exponential suppression is negligible; discharge is rapid. For smaller \(Q\), the exponent grows and discharge slows.

13. Euclidean Action and Partition Function

The Euclidean RN metric (with \(t \to -i\tau\)):

\begin{equation} ds_E^2 = f(r) d\tau^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f(r)} + r^2 d\Omega^2 \end{equation}

Regularity at \(r_+\) requires \(\tau \sim \tau + \beta\) with \(\beta = 2\pi/\kappa = 1/T_H\).

The Euclidean action:

\begin{equation} I_E = -\frac{1}{16\pi G} \int d^4x \sqrt{g} R + \frac{1}{4} \int d^4x \sqrt{g} F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \text{boundary terms} \end{equation}

For RN, the bulk action vanishes on-shell (\(R = 0\) for Einstein-Maxwell). The boundary term at infinity gives:

\begin{equation} I_E = \beta M - \frac{A_+}{4G} - \beta \Phi_+ Q \end{equation}

The partition function \(Z = e^{-I_E}\) yields the thermodynamic potentials.

14. Dual CFT Description (Kerr/CFT Analogue)

For near-extremal RN, the near-horizon \(\text{AdS}_2 \times S^2\) suggests a dual 1D CFT. The central charge:

\begin{equation} c = \frac{3 r_e^2}{G} = 12 \pi G M^2 \end{equation}

The Frolov-Thorne temperature:

\begin{equation} T_L = \frac{r_+^2}{2\pi (r_+ - r_-)} \end{equation}

Cardy formula reproduces the entropy:

\begin{equation} S = \frac{\pi^2}{3} c T_L = \frac{\pi^2}{3} \cdot \frac{3 r_+^2}{G} \cdot \frac{r_+^2}{2\pi (r_+ - r_-)} \cdot \frac{r_+ - r_-}{r_+^2} = \frac{\pi r_+^2}{G} \end{equation}

15. Perturbative Stability

15.1 Gravitational Perturbations

The Regge-Wheeler equation for axial perturbations:

\begin{equation} \frac{d^2 \Psi}{dr_*^2} + \left[ \omega^2 - V_{\text{RW}}(r) \right] \Psi = 0 \end{equation}

where \(dr_* = dr/f(r)\) and

\begin{equation} V_{\text{RW}}(r) = f(r) \left[ \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} - \frac{6GM}{r^3} + \frac{4GQ^2}{r^4} \right] \end{equation}

15.2 Electromagnetic Perturbations

Coupled to gravitational perturbations via the Zerilli equation. The system is stable for \(Q^2 < G M^2\).

16. Geodesics and Particle Motion

16.1 Effective Potential for Timelike Geodesics

For a particle of mass \(\mu\) and charge \(q\):

\begin{equation} \frac{1}{2} \dot{r}^2 + V_{\text{eff}}(r) = \frac{E^2}{2} \end{equation}

\begin{equation} V_{\text{eff}}(r) = \frac{f(r)}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{L^2}{r^2} \right) - \frac{qQ}{r} \end{equation}

16.2 Innermost Stable Circular Orbit (ISCO)

Solve \(V_{\text{eff}}' = V_{\text{eff}}'' = 0\). For neutral particles (\(q=0\)):

\begin{equation} r_{\text{ISCO}} = \frac{GM}{2} \left[ 3 + \sqrt{9 - 8\frac{Q^2}{GM^2}} \right] \end{equation}

18. Appendix: Derivation of Smarr Formula from Scaling

The RN solution is characterized by two parameters \((M, Q)\). Under scaling \(r \to \lambda r\), \(t \to \lambda t\):

\begin{itemize} \item \(M \to \lambda M\) \item \(Q \to \lambda Q\) \item \(A \to \lambda^2 A\) \item \(\kappa \to \lambda^{-1} \kappa\) \item \(\Phi \to \lambda^{-1} \Phi\) \end{itemize}

Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions: \(M(\lambda A, \lambda Q) = \lambda M(A, Q)\). Differentiate w.r.t. \(\lambda\) at \(\lambda=1\):

\begin{equation} M = \left( \frac{\partial M}{\partial A} \right)_Q A + \left( \frac{\partial M}{\partial Q} \right)_A Q \end{equation}

From the first law \(dM = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi G} dA + \Phi dQ\), we identify:

\begin{equation} \frac{\partial M}{\partial A} = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi G}, \quad \frac{\partial M}{\partial Q} = \Phi \end{equation}

Thus:

\begin{equation} M = \frac{\kappa A}{8\pi G} + \Phi Q = 2 T_H S + \Phi Q \end{equation}
