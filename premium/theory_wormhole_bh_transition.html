Wormhole–black-hole transitions

Can a black hole become a wormhole, or vice versa? This page surveys the theoretical possibilities: topology change, the ER=EPR conjecture, and the observational signatures that would distinguish them.

1. Topology change in general relativity

Classical GR does not allow topology change without singularities or violations of the energy conditions. A black hole (simply connected, one asymptotic region) cannot continuously become a wormhole (two asymptotic regions connected by a throat) without either a singularity or exotic matter. Quantum gravity may allow such transitions via instantons (e.g. Giddings-Strominger wormholes).

2. ER = EPR

The Maldacena-Susskind conjecture (ER=EPR) identifies Einstein-Rosen bridges (wormholes) with Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen entanglement. In this picture, two entangled black holes are connected by a non-traversable wormhole; the wormhole is a geometric manifestation of quantum entanglement. This suggests a deep link but does not imply traversability.

3. Black-hole-to-wormhole transitions via quantum effects

Some proposals (e.g. Hayward, Haggard-Rovelli) suggest that quantum effects near the Planck scale could convert a black hole into a white hole or a wormhole, avoiding the singularity. The transition time would be of order the evaporation time (~M³) or shorter. These are speculative and not derived from a complete theory.

4. Observational discriminants

  • Shadow shape: a wormhole shadow can differ from a Kerr shadow (e.g. asymmetric, or with a central bright spot from the other side).
  • Gravitational waves: a wormhole merger would produce a different ringdown (possibly with echoes).
  • Lensing: a wormhole can produce multiple images with characteristic magnification patterns.

No such signatures have been observed; all constraints are upper bounds.

5. Assumptions and caveats

  • All transition scenarios require quantum gravity; none is derived from first principles.
  • Exotic matter (negative energy) is needed for traversable wormholes; its existence is unproven.
  • Observational discriminants are model-dependent and degenerate with Kerr uncertainties.

6. References

  • Maldacena J., Susskind L., 2013, Fortsch. Phys. 61, 781 — ER=EPR.
  • Haggard H. M., Rovelli C., 2015, Phys. Rev. D 92, 104020 — black-to-white hole transition.
  • Cardoso V. et al., 2016, Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 171101 — echoes.
